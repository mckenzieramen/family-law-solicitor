What is spousal maintenance?

When a relationship ends, one person may be left unable to meet their reasonable living expenses while the other has the means to help. Spousal maintenance is the financial support the law provides for in that situation.

It is separate from both child support and property settlement, although in practice it is often resolved at the same time as property. It applies to married and de facto couples alike, including same-sex couples.

Whether you are seeking support or have received a claim, Prevail Family Law can advise you on whether maintenance is likely to be payable, how much, and for how long.

When can spousal maintenance be ordered?

There is no automatic right to spousal maintenance. Two things must be shown:

  • The person seeking it can’t reasonably support themselves. Common reasons include caring for a child of the relationship who is under 18, age or ill health, or another adequate reason, such as a long time out of paid work.
  • The other party has the capacity to pay. This is measured against their income, assets and own reasonable living expenses.

Both must be met. If either one isn’t, no order will be made.

What the Court considers

In deciding whether maintenance should be paid, and how much, the Court looks at the circumstances of each party, including:

  • their age and state of health
  • their income, property and financial resources, and their capacity to earn
  • whether either of them cares for children of the relationship
  • how long the relationship lasted and how it affected each party’s earning capacity
  • what standard of living is reasonable in the circumstances
  • the economic impact of any family violence
  • whether either party is financially supported by, or supporting, another person
  • the terms of any property settlement.

There is no set formula. Every case turns on its own facts.

How spousal maintenance is paid

Spousal maintenance can take the form of:

  • Periodic payments. For example, weekly or monthly, for a fixed term or until a specified event.
  • A lump sum. Often built into a property settlement.
  • Payment of particular expenses. Such as the mortgage, rent or school fees.

It is generally designed to bridge a period of transition, for example while someone retrains, returns to work or waits for the children to start school, rather than to continue indefinitely.

Urgent and interim maintenance

If separation has left you without enough to cover your immediate living costs, you can ask the Court for urgent maintenance before your case is finalised. The Court can make interim orders for support until the final hearing, or until a property settlement is reached.

Time limits

Strict time limits apply to spousal maintenance applications:

  • Married couples. Within 12 months after the divorce takes effect.
  • De facto couples. Within 2 years after separation.

Applying after that requires the Court’s permission, which is granted only in limited circumstances. Don’t leave it until the deadline is close: get advice early.

Varying or ending spousal maintenance

A spousal maintenance order can be changed if there is a significant change in either party’s circumstances, such as a change in income, health or caring responsibilities.

Spousal maintenance usually ends when:

  • the period fixed by the order or agreement ends
  • the person receiving it marries or remarries, unless the Court orders otherwise
  • either party dies.

Formalising spousal maintenance

If you and your former partner agree on spousal maintenance, the arrangement can be made binding through:

  • Consent orders. These are approved by the Court and can be enforced if payments stop.
  • A financial agreement. This can also release both of you from any future spousal maintenance claim.

If you can’t agree, you can apply to the Court for spousal maintenance orders. Before doing so, you will usually need to make a genuine attempt to resolve the dispute and exchange financial disclosure.

Frequently Asked Questions

Find answers to common questions about spousal maintenance.

Is spousal maintenance the same as child support?

No. Child support is paid for the children and is usually assessed by Services Australia. Spousal maintenance supports a former partner and is settled either by agreement or by the Court.

How long does spousal maintenance last?

It depends on the circumstances. It is commonly limited to a set period to help someone become financially independent, but it may continue for longer in some cases, such as after a long relationship or where there is ongoing ill health.

Can I claim spousal maintenance if I’m working?

Possibly. If your income doesn’t cover your reasonable needs and your former partner can afford to contribute, you may still be eligible.

Does a new relationship make a difference?

It can. If the person receiving maintenance is being financially supported by a new partner, that is relevant to whether they still need support.

Can I give up my right to spousal maintenance?

Yes, through a financial agreement or, in some cases, consent orders. Get advice before you give up a claim, as it usually can’t be revived later.

What if my former partner stops paying?

If there is a court order, you can apply to the Court to enforce it and recover the unpaid amounts.
