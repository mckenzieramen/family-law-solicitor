What child support is for

After separation, both parents remain responsible for supporting their children financially. Child support is how that responsibility is shared. It helps cover everyday costs such as food, clothing, housing, schooling and activities. There are three main ways to organise it: a private arrangement, an assessment by Services Australia, or a formal child support agreement. Prevail Family Law can help you work out which option suits your family, and put it in place properly.

Private arrangements

Some parents agree on child support between themselves, without involving Services Australia. This can work well where you communicate easily, trust each other, and payments are made in full and on time. The risk is that an informal arrangement can’t be enforced. If payments stop or one parent’s circumstances change, there is nothing to fall back on. If you go down this path, put the arrangement in writing, keep records of every payment, and think about whether a formal agreement would give you more security.

Child support assessments through Services Australia

If you can’t agree, or would rather not negotiate directly, either parent, or a non-parent carer such as a grandparent, can apply to Services Australia for a child support assessment. You can apply online, by phone or by form. The assessment uses a set formula that takes into account:

  • each parent’s taxable income
  • the number and ages of the children
  • how much care each parent provides.

An assessment is separate from parenting arrangements in the Family Court, although the amount of care each parent has will affect the result. Child support usually ends when a child turns 18. If a child turns 18 during their final year of school, you can apply to have child support continue until the end of that school year, but the application must be made before the child turns 18. Child support can also end earlier, for example if the child marries, enters a de facto relationship, or is adopted.

Disagree with your assessment? You can ask Services Australia to review it. If you’re still not satisfied, you may be able to seek a further review through the Administrative Review Tribunal. In special circumstances, such as high costs for a child’s care or schooling, you can also apply to change the assessment.

Paying and collecting child support

Once an assessment is in place, the receiving parent can choose how payments are made:

  • Private Collect. Parents arrange payment directly between themselves. Services Australia still calculates the amount.
  • Child Support Collect (also called Agency Collect). Services Australia collects payments from the paying parent and passes them on. This can include deductions from wages or Centrelink payments.

Services Australia can only collect and enforce amounts that are part of its own system. It can’t enforce an informal private arrangement.

When payments aren’t made

If child support is being collected by Services Australia and the paying parent falls behind, Services Australia has a range of enforcement powers. These include:

  • deductions from wages or Centrelink payments
  • deductions from bank accounts
  • taking tax refunds to cover the debt
  • stopping the paying parent from leaving Australia until the debt is dealt with.

Keep a record of missed payments, including bank statements and any messages about payment. If those steps don’t recover the money, you can apply to the Federal Circuit and Family Court of Australia for enforcement orders. The Court can make orders such as seizing or selling property, and can award interest and costs. Prevail Family Law can advise you on whether court action is worthwhile and run the application for you.

Child support agreements

Parents can also make a formal child support agreement and have it accepted by Services Australia. There are two types.

Binding child support agreements

A binding agreement sets out how much child support is paid and how. It can also cover particular expenses, such as school fees, medical costs or extracurricular activities.

  • Advantages: certainty, flexibility in what it covers, and it can be enforced.
  • Disadvantages: it is very hard to change or end if circumstances change. It generally needs a new binding agreement or a court order.
  • Legal advice is compulsory. Each parent must get independent legal advice before signing, and each lawyer must sign a certificate confirming that advice was given.

Because a binding agreement can last for years and is difficult to undo, it should only be made after careful thought and proper advice.

Limited child support agreements

A limited agreement is simpler and doesn’t require legal advice. It can be cheaper and quicker to put in place.

  • There must already be a child support assessment.
  • The amount agreed must be at least the amount in the assessment.
  • It can be ended by either parent after three years, or earlier in some situations, such as a significant change in the assessed amount.
  • It is generally less detailed than a binding agreement, which can lead to misunderstandings.

Child support after 18

Child support under the formula generally stops at 18, but some children still need support beyond then because of a disability. In these cases, a parent or the adult child can apply to the Court for adult child maintenance. Prevail Family Law can advise on whether this applies to your family.

Changes ahead

The Australian Government has announced changes to the child support system, with most planned to start from 1 July 2027 once legislation has passed. They are aimed at making child support easier to collect, including through wage deductions, and at stopping the system from being used to harass or pressure the other parent. Prevail Family Law keeps up to date with these changes and can explain how they may affect you.

Frequently Asked Questions

How is child support worked out?

Services Australia uses a formula based on both parents’ incomes, the number and ages of the children, and how much care each parent has. You can get an estimate using the Child Support Estimator on the Services Australia website.

Do we have to use Services Australia?

No. You can make a private arrangement or a formal agreement instead. A limited agreement does need an assessment to be in place first.

What if my ex-partner earns cash or hides income?

Services Australia can investigate, and in some cases the assessment can be changed to reflect a parent’s real earning capacity. Get advice early, and keep any evidence of their income or lifestyle.

Can child support cover school fees?

Yes. A binding child support agreement can set out who pays for private school fees and other specific costs. In some cases, high schooling costs can also be grounds to change an assessment.

Does child support change if care arrangements change?

Yes. The amount of care each parent provides is part of the formula, so tell Services Australia promptly if care changes.

Can I stop paying if I’m not seeing my children?

No. Child support and time with children are separate. Stopping payments can lead to enforcement action. If you’re not seeing your children, get advice about parenting arrangements instead.

Can a binding agreement be changed?

Usually only by making a new binding agreement, or by a court order in limited circumstances, such as a significant change in circumstances that would cause hardship.

What happens when my child turns 18?

Child support under the formula generally ends, unless you applied before their 18th birthday to continue it to the end of the school year. Adult child maintenance may be available in some situations.

Does divorce affect child support?

No. Child support is separate from divorce and property settlement. You can apply for an assessment at any time after separation, whether or not you are divorced. See Divorce →
